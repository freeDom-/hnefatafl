# 0.31.0 - Optimized alpha-beta search and portable tournament setup

This stable feature release makes the **Jarl alpha-beta search do less work at
equal depth** with a bounded, depth-aware transposition table, improved move
ordering and root-window pruning, exposes the **optional D4 symmetry
optimizations and the evaluator heuristic** through the API, tournament setup and
records, and adds **portable tournament configuration import/export** plus
**training-job rename**. The previous stable milestone `0.30.0` is already tagged
and published; nothing was overwritten or moved.

The search changes return the same minimax values as the historical reference on
every measured fixed-depth position. They are a speed and work-avoidance change,
not a model-quality or playing-strength change; see
[Limitations](#limitations).

## Delivered behavior

### Optimized alpha-beta search

- Each search owns one bounded, depth-aware transposition table for its lifetime
  (`hnefatafl/ai.py`). Entries store the searched depth, an exact/lower/upper
  bound, a preferred move, a generation and the canonical orientation. Replacement
  prefers deeper entries and exact bounds within the configured capacity, and an
  interrupted search never publishes a partial exact result. The table is never
  shared and is discarded when the move ends.
- The ordinary key is now `(ruleset, board, turn, repetition signature)`, where the
  signature is the order-independent occurrence counts of board/turn keys. The
  engine's threefold rule and the Shield maiden repetition penalty depend only on
  those counts, so the compact key preserves every future outcome while letting
  move-order transpositions share one entry. Histories with different counts keep
  distinct keys, so repetition-sensitive states never alias.
- The root no longer searches every child with a full window. It narrows the window
  with the best value found so far, and the first move to reach the best value
  wins, which stays deterministic. An isolated A/B on the 37-position corpus cut
  median interior nodes about 1.9× and median leaf evaluations about 7.3× with a
  smaller and noisier ~1.3× median wall-time reduction and identical values. It is
  always on and is not a user-facing switch.
- Move ordering applies one deterministic priority: principal variation,
  transposition move, killers, history, then stable static order. History is
  consulted even when no preferred move exists, and sorting is skipped when every
  history score is zero.
- Optional D4-canonical transpositions and full-state symmetry move pruning are
  available for heuristic-backed search. Move pruning computes the full-state
  stabilizer, partitions the legal moves into orbits and retains one deterministic
  representative per orbit *before* any successor state is built, so discarded
  moves cost no `apply_move` or static evaluation. Because the stabilizer uses the
  complete state (board, turn and ordered history), a visually symmetric board with
  asymmetric history is never pruned. The Shield maiden evaluator is D4-invariant;
  requesting symmetry reuse with a neural evaluator raises instead of merging
  unequal predictions. Both features stay opt-in and off by default.
- The monotonic deadline is observed inside move ordering, canonicalization and
  symmetry pruning through a cooperative check callback. A long-history
  canonicalization or stabilizer aborts promptly instead of overrunning the budget.
- The historical exact-value-cache search remains available as a reference through
  `legacy: true`. The public `choose_move` contract (immutable `State` in, one legal
  `Move` out, monotonic deadline, legal fallback) is unchanged.
- Alpha-beta diagnostics now expose visited nodes, evaluations, cutoffs,
  successors, transposition exact/bound/cutoff counts, cross-orientation reuse,
  symmetry skips, per-node timing and iterative-deepening interruption counters.
  See [AI opponents](../AI.md#search-optimization-configuration).

### Configurable search optimization and evaluator heuristic

- A nested `search` object is accepted by the game, advisor and tournament APIs and
  is validated and normalized server-side by `clean_search_config` in
  `hnefatafl/ai.py`. It selects `legacy`, `transposition`, `move_ordering`,
  `symmetry_transposition`, `symmetry_pruning` and `table_capacity` (1–500,000,
  default 100,000). Malformed settings are rejected atomically, and `search` is
  rejected for any algorithm other than alpha-beta (or a neural alpha-beta
  wrapper).
- Tournament setup exposes the same options for a custom alpha-beta opponent under
  **Alpha-beta optimizations**, with **Legacy alpha-beta**, **Optimized default**,
  **Full symmetry** and **Custom** presets plus individual toggles and a bounded
  table capacity (`frontend/src/alphaBetaSearch.ts`). Jarl remains a fixed preset
  without editable optimization controls.
- Jarl (alpha-beta) and Seer (MCTS) now reuse the Shield maiden child scoring for
  their model-free search, including move ordering and the alpha-beta leaf/rollout
  horizon. A custom alpha-beta or MCTS opponent may set a top-level `heuristic` to
  `shield_maiden` (default) or `shield_bearer` for comparison; the standalone
  `baseline` algorithm is always Shield bearer, and neural search uses its model
  value head. New-game custom opponents, tournament setup and custom advisors
  expose the same choice (`frontend/src/HeuristicSelect.tsx`).
- An explicit nested `search` object and the resolved `heuristic` are part of the
  effective configuration identity. A record that names the defaults explicitly
  stays a distinct, stable identity from one without the object, so historical
  records are never silently merged and their runes and statistics do not move.
  See [Persistent identity runes](../IDENTITIES.md).
- Records show the resolved heuristic and an **Alpha-beta optimizations** summary
  that reflects the effective search rather than stale switches
  (`frontend/src/RecordsParameters.tsx`).

### Portable tournament configuration import and export

- **Import configuration** in the tournament setup dialog reads a portable JSON
  document mirroring the `POST /api/tournaments` request body and fills the
  editable form. It never starts a tournament and never restores server-managed
  values: IDs, status, ownership, progress, results, timestamps, worker paths and
  runes are ignored. A failed import leaves the current form unchanged and names
  the offending fields.
- A nested `search` object on a custom alpha-beta opponent is validated and
  normalized to the same shape as the API, so it populates the form, submits,
  persists, executes and re-imports without loss. A custom alpha-beta or MCTS
  opponent may also set `heuristic`. Neural symmetry reuse, a `search` object for a
  non-alpha-beta algorithm and missing model references are rejected as a whole.
- **Export results** downloads the saved tournament as JSON. The top level uses the
  same importable configuration schema and a nested `results` object carries the
  status, timestamps, progress, standings and match records for review. Because
  the configuration fields are at the top level, an exported file can be imported
  again as a fresh tournament; server-managed identifiers, worker PIDs and runes
  are never included (`hnefatafl/tournaments.py`, `GET /api/tournaments/{id}/export`).
- A complete legacy-versus-full-symmetry comparison is shipped as
  [`configs/alpha-beta-search-comparison.json`](../../configs/alpha-beta-search-comparison.json);
  the setup dialog can also create the same tournament entirely through the
  browser. See [Tournaments](../TOURNAMENTS.md#import-and-export-configurations).

### Training-job rename and workshop polish

- **Rename job** edits the display name only, for active and inactive jobs. The
  dialog starts from the current name; saving persists a separate display field and
  refreshes the rail and header without a page reload, while cancelling keeps the
  previous name. Empty, whitespace-only and longer-than-100-character names are
  rejected on both the client and the server, and a failed save keeps the entered
  text. Job IDs, output paths, checkpoints, parameters, worker assignments and
  execution state never change (`PATCH /api/training/jobs/{id}`,
  `frontend/src/JobRenameDialog.tsx`).
- The tournament **Match log** starts collapsed whenever a tournament is opened.
  A live data refresh of the same running tournament keeps the current expanded or
  collapsed state, while opening a different tournament collapses it again.
- New training jobs default to **random D4 symmetry augmentation** and a
  **10-second** MCTS/evaluation time ceiling. Jobs and checkpoints that predate
  either setting keep their historical `off`/2-second behavior unless explicitly
  overridden. See [Training](../TRAINING.md#training-symmetry-augmentation).

### Benchmark tooling and measured evidence

- New `benchmark-alpha-beta` CLI command (`hnefatafl/alpha_beta_benchmark.py`)
  drives the real production `choose_move` path on a deterministic corpus of
  openings, seeded midgames, symmetric/asymmetric positions, immediate wins, king
  threats, restricted-square tactics, Copenhagen shieldwalls/encirclement/forts,
  repetition-sensitive positions and long histories. It runs a fixed-depth
  correctness pass against the legacy reference, an independent exhaustive minimax
  oracle, equal-budget fixed-time scenarios, memory tracing and ordering/root-window
  ablations. `--summary-output` writes a compact summary whose `source_sha256`
  names the revision that produced it.
- `scripts/alpha_beta_strength.py` plays the shipped comparison configuration at
  equal time budgets and reports wins/draws/losses, a normal-approximation 95%
  interval and mean completed depth.
- Raw reports and interpretation are checked in under
  [Phase 4 symmetry evidence](../benchmarks/alpha-beta-symmetry.md),
  [control-refinement evidence](../benchmarks/alpha-beta-controls.md) and
  [profiling evidence](../benchmarks/alpha-beta-profiling.md).

## Changes and fixes

- Fixed the tournament name label spacing from its input.
- Corrected the tournament details view and made the match log start collapsed.
- Replaced the full ordered-history transposition key with the compact,
  order-independent repetition signature and classified bounds against the window
  that was actually searched after table tightening.
- Reduced the transposition-table capacity ceiling from 5,000,000 to 500,000
  because a repetition-aware key grows with history.
- Corrected `transposition_cross_orientation_hits` to count only probes whose
  stored orientation differs from the query's canonical orientation, and added
  `transposition_successful_lookups`.
- Applied history-based move ordering even when no preferred move exists, and
  skipped sorting when every history score is zero.
- Job rename, tournament export and nested search settings are covered by new
  backend tests (`tests/test_alpha_beta_search.py`, `tests/test_job_rename_api.py`)
  and frontend tests (`alphaBetaSearch.test.ts`, `tournamentConfigImport.test.ts`,
  `JobRename.test.tsx`, `trainingDefaults.test.ts`).

## Upgrade notes

No storage, database, checkpoint or protocol migration is required.

- New training jobs default to `training_symmetry_mode: "random_d4"` and a
  10-second MCTS/evaluation ceiling. Existing jobs and checkpoints that predate
  either setting keep their historical `off`/2-second behavior, including through
  resume and the API, unless you set the value explicitly.
- Existing games, tournaments, models and remote workers are unchanged. Existing
  tournament configurations without a `search` object keep their historical
  identity and use the optimized alpha-beta default; records are never rewritten.
- Remote workers need no update for the search, tournament or rename features.
  Update workers only to keep their advertised version current.
- Re-importing a tournament export starts a new tournament; it does not restore
  progress, IDs or results into the original record.

## Limitations

- The search optimizations are a work-avoidance change. The measured 20-game
  equal-time comparison did not show a statistically significant strength
  difference, and full symmetry reached a slightly *shallower* mean completed
  depth because canonicalization consumes part of the fixed budget. Speed is not
  reported as strength.
- D4-canonical transpositions and symmetry move pruning remain opt-in and off by
  default: they are large wins on symmetric positions but cost roughly 7–25% on
  generic asymmetric midgames, and canonicalization cost grows with board size and
  history length.
- The ordinary transposition table is correct and finds entries, but every hit was
  depth-incompatible under iterative deepening in the measured corpus, so it is
  compatibility overhead rather than a measured speedup; it is required for the
  optional canonical-transposition feature.
- Eager successor construction in `_ordered` dominates the remaining wall time and
  is shared with the legacy search. Lazy child generation was recorded as the main
  remaining opportunity but not implemented.
- The benchmark corpus is deterministic but not exhaustive, and the fixed-time
  probe is a small illustration rather than a statistical strength test. Absolute
  wall times are specific to the single WSL2 CPU host.
- Browser validation is Chromium desktop/mobile emulation, not physical Safari.
  CUDA, MPS and OpenVINO GPU execution were not exercised for this release.
